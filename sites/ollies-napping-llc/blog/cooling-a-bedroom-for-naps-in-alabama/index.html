How to cool a bedroom for a nap in Alabama heat

Anyone who has tried to nap in July around Southside and Gadsden knows the problem. The room is warm, the air is heavy, and a nap that should take twenty minutes turns into an hour of tossing. Cooling a bedroom here is not about buying the biggest air conditioner on the shelf. It is about keeping the heat out in the first place and moving the air you already have.

Start with the window. Most of the heat in a bedroom comes through the glass in the afternoon. A light blocking curtain or even a plain shade that pulls all the way down makes a real difference, and it darkens the room at the same time, which helps you fall asleep faster. If the sun hits that window in the afternoon, keep it covered from midday until evening.

Next, get the air moving. A ceiling fan running the right direction pushes cool air down and makes a room feel several degrees cooler than the thermostat says. Most ceiling fans have a switch on the motor housing to reverse the blades. In summer you want the blades turning counterclockwise when you look up at them. A plain box fan in the corner works too, and it is quieter than most people expect.

Then think about when you run the air. Cooling a room takes time, so drop the thermostat a little half an hour before you lie down rather than after. If you keep the rest of the house warmer and shut the bedroom door, you are cooling one room instead of the whole place, and that costs less. In our humidity, the dry setting on a window unit or a small dehumidifier helps too, because damp air feels warmer than dry air at the same temperature.

What you sleep on matters as much as the air. Cotton sheets breathe, and a heavy comforter traps heat. A light cotton blanket is plenty for a summer nap. Keep the pillow simple and skip the flannel. If you have been outside working in the heat, rinse off with a lukewarm shower first. It sounds backwards, but a lukewarm shower cools you down more than a cold one, and it washes off the sweat and pollen that make a bed feel sticky.

Timing helps as well. The hottest part of an Alabama afternoon is usually between about two and five, so a nap right after lunch tends to be cooler than one late in the day. It also fits the way most people get sleepy in the early afternoon, and it leaves plenty of room before bedtime.

One more thing worth saying: keep the phone and the television out of reach. A dark, cool, quiet room does its job quickly. A room with a screen in it turns a twenty minute nap into a long scroll through the news.
